Understanding Solar Eclipses

A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon passes directly between the Earth and the Sun, temporarily blocking all or part of the Sun's light from reaching Earth.

Types of Solar Eclipses
  • Total Eclipse: The Moon completely covers the Sun's bright disk, revealing the faint outer atmosphere (corona).
  • Partial Eclipse: The Moon and Sun are not perfectly aligned, creating a crescent-shaped Sun.
  • Annular Eclipse: The Moon is at apogee (farther from Earth) and appears smaller than the Sun, creating a "ring of fire".
  • Hybrid Eclipse: Shifts between an annular and total eclipse along different parts of its path.
Shadow Dynamics

The Moon casts two main shadows onto Earth during an alignment:

  • Umbra: The dark inner core shadow. Observers within this zone experience a total solar eclipse.
  • Penumbra: The lighter outer shadow region where observers see a partial solar eclipse.
Orbital Mechanics

Earth revolves around the Sun in an anticlockwise direction (when viewed from above the North Pole). Solar eclipses do not occur every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted at approximately 5 degrees relative to Earth's orbital plane (the ecliptic).

Viewing Safety

Never look directly at the Sun without ISO 12312-2 certified solar viewing glasses. Standard sunglasses are not safe for direct solar observation.
